Questions to ask any wealth firm

  • Are you a fiduciary at all times, in writing?
  • How exactly are you paid — and by whom?
  • What is my all-in annual cost, including fund fees?
  • Who holds my assets (the custodian)?
  • Do you sell proprietary products?
  • How will you measure success for me?

FAQ

Do I need to be rich to use a wealth manager?

No. Minimums vary widely. Many fee-only planners work on flat or hourly fees with no minimum, while traditional private wealth firms often start somewhere around the high six or low seven figures.

What's the difference between a private bank and a wealth manager?

A private bank combines banking and lending with investment services under one relationship manager. A wealth manager focuses on investment and planning advice and may hold your assets at an independent custodian.

Are private equity and private credit suitable for individuals?

They can play a small role for investors who understand the illiquidity, fees and complexity, and who meet any eligibility rules. Most individuals are well served by low-cost public-market portfolios.
