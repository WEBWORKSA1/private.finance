How this calculator works

The 50/30/20 rule divides after-tax income into three buckets: about half for needs (housing, utilities, groceries, insurance, minimum debt payments, transport to work), about 30% for wants (dining out, subscriptions, travel, hobbies) and about 20% for savings and extra debt repayment. It is a starting framework, not a law — in high-cost cities, needs can legitimately exceed 50%, and aggressive savers may flip wants and savings.

Adjust the sliders to your own split, then enter what you actually spend to get specific feedback on where the gap is.

Frequently asked questions

Is 50/30/20 based on gross or net income?

Net (take-home) income — what lands in your account after taxes and payroll deductions. If you contribute to a workplace retirement plan before tax, you can count that toward the 20%.

What if my needs are over 50%?

That is common in expensive housing markets. Reduce wants first, then look for structural fixes such as a roommate, refinancing or a cheaper commute.

Where should the 20% go?

Typically in order: a starter emergency fund, any employer match, high-interest debt, a full emergency fund, then long-term investing.
