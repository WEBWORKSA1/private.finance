How this calculator works

An emergency fund covers essential expenses — not your full lifestyle — if income stops or a large unexpected bill arrives. The calculator totals the essentials you enter and multiplies by your chosen months of coverage. A common guideline is three to six months; people with variable income, a single household income or dependants often aim for six to twelve.

If the target feels huge, start with a mini-goal of one month, or a fixed starter amount, then keep building while you pay down high-interest debt.

Frequently asked questions

Should I invest my emergency fund?

Generally no. Its job is stability and access, not growth. High-interest savings accounts, money-market funds or short-term deposits are typical homes for it.

Do I need one if I have credit cards?

Credit is not a safety net — it turns an emergency into high-interest debt. A cash buffer protects your credit score and your choices.

What counts as an emergency?

Job loss, urgent medical or dental costs, essential car or home repairs, and urgent travel. Planned expenses like holidays or annual bills belong in separate sinking funds.
